Our repository follows strict KISS principles—relying exclusively on Python standard library modules for core calculations so that any stranger can run the full pipeline in under half a second.

The architecture consists of four primary modules:
1. mandate.py: Encodes the CIO's rules from the Meridian Foundation Reserve Mandate—specifically the derived 3.0% reserve floor (S3), the 90% five-day liquidity threshold (S8), and the 8% single corporate issuer limit (S6).
2. portfolio.py: Contains our multi-asset financial mathematics. It validates the correlation matrix, computes covariance, calculates baselines, and runs our discrete 5-point grid search.
3. run_project2.py: Our deterministic CLI runner. It imports mandate.py and portfolio.py directly with zero duplicated math, resolving file paths relative to the script.
4. app.py: Our interactive Streamlit web dashboard.
Data flows from prices.csv—holding 60 monthly observations for BIL, IEF, LQD, and VT—and asof.py, which implements point-in-time FRED/ALFRED ingestion.

In portfolio.py:
First, validate_correlation_matrix at line 73: Verifies that the matrix is square, bounded between minus one and positive one, symmetric within 10^-6, and has a unit diagonal; otherwise it raises a descriptive ValueError and refuses to execute.
Second, build_covariance_matrix at line 124: Transforms pairwise correlations and volatilities into covariance space via Sigma_ij = rho_ij * sigma_i * sigma_j.
Third, search_lowest_risk_mix at line 258: Evaluates discrete 5% long-only asset combinations summing to 100%. It filters for mixes satisfying the 6.50% return hurdle and 3.0% reserve minimum, then selects the combination that minimizes annualized portfolio volatility.

Now let's demonstrate execution live in the terminal.
I run our baseline command:
python3 run_project2.py

In 0.28 seconds, it audits our candidates: Candidate A passes, but Candidate E—the 60/40 benchmark—fails because its reserve weight is 0.0%. It computes our baselines and outputs our constrained portfolio: 0% Bills, 20% 10-Year, 25% IG, and 55% Equity, delivering 6.51% return and 9.44% volatility—102 basis points below the benchmark.

Now, I execute our Locked Changed-Input test, which was committed to GitHub prior to execution:
python3 run_project2.py --cost-bps 50

Notice the result: Net sleeve returns drop by 50 basis points across the board. Because the 6.50% hurdle remains binding, the optimizer is forced to shift 15 percentage points—or fifteen million dollars—out of fixed income into equities, moving equity from 55% to 70%. 
This cuts our reserve buffer from 20% to 10% and spikes volatility from 9.44% to 11.57%—erasing our risk advantage and making the portfolio 111 basis points riskier than the benchmark. The console flags: PASS MANDATE / UNSTABLE.

Finally, our highest-risk unresolved limitation is Statement S9: 'cannot have a calendar-year loss greater than 10%.' 
As encoded in mandate.py, realized loss is an ex-post stochastic return outcome, not a static constraint at t=0. A 55% equity portfolio can easily experience greater than 10% drawdown in a bear market. Treating S9 as a static pass/fail check gives false security; it requires formal ex-ante VaR/CVaR modeling.

To govern this risk, our system enforces an automated Process Stop Condition: if an optimization run triggers a weight shift over 10 points, breaches our 40%–65% equity policy band, or if the BAA10Y credit spread exceeds 250 basis points, automated rebalancing halts immediately and escalates to the committee.
