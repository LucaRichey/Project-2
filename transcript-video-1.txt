I initially assumed a standard Markowitz optimizer would produce an adoptable allocation for the Meridian Foundation. 

Two findings changed my thinking:
First, auditing the client’s own 60/40 benchmark—Candidate E in mandate.py—revealed that it FAILS Rule S3 of its own mandate. Holding 40% in corporate bonds and 60% in equities leaves 0.0% in reserve-eligible government paper, violating the 3.0% cash reserve floor.
Second, in our changed-input stress testing, adding a realistic 50 basis point fee drag caused the optimizer to shift 15 percentage points—or fifteen million dollars—straight into equities, spiking volatility from 9.44% to 11.57% and wiping out our benchmark risk advantage. 
My system therefore evolved from a simple optimizer into a multi-layered stability engine with point-in-time data gates and an explicit Process Stop Condition.


For my AI-output check, the AI originally proposed formulating our portfolio as an unconstrained continuous quadratic program using scipy's SLSQP, yielding fractional weights like 54.819234% in equities. 

I audited and REJECTED this output. In institutional reserve management, micro-percentage precision is economically meaningless and triggers excessive rebalancing turnover. I corrected it by implementing a discrete 5-point grid search in portfolio.py, generating clean, tradeable 5% allocations—yielding our baseline candidate of 0% Bills, 20% 10-Year, 25% IG, and 55% Equity. 

Similarly, when the AI suggested using unlagged Nonfarm Payrolls, our point-in-time vintage audit revealed 381 historical revisions in PAYEMS. I rejected payrolls and admitted market-clearing credit spread BAA10Y with a strict 1-day as-of lag.


Because raw optimization without policy bands produces dangerous capital shifts, I recommend: PILOT, DO NOT ADOPT. Authorize a ten-million-dollar pilot, and keep ninety million in canonical sleeves until policy bands and shrinkage priors are codified. Thank you.
